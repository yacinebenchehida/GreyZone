THE POPULATION GREY ZONE

Genetic evidence
can lag behind life.

After a split, chance changes allele frequencies independently on each side. This is genetic drift. In large populations, those changes are often small, so a genetic study can struggle to detect the separation.

The grey zone depends on population sizes, gene flow, time, and the study’s sample size and markers. There is no universal generation when it ends.

A little more about the model

This draft uses two neutral diploid Wright–Fisher populations with constant sizes and unlinked biallelic markers. Their sizes add to the ancestral total, and both start with exactly matching allele frequencies. No selection, mutation, sex structure or spatial effects are included. The sharks are illustrative representatives, not a biological model of shark reproduction or behaviour.

Migration mixes frequencies before drift: Nm migrants in each direction means m = Nm/N for the receiving population. Animated crossings illustrate that exchange; their count is not a census of migrants. Any positive migration means isolation is incomplete.

FST is Hudson’s ratio of summed diversities. Samples use independent gene-copy draws and an approximate multilocus Pearson chi-square test at α = 0.05. Rare alleles and small samples can affect its calibration. Power is estimated from 80 independent histories and fresh samples every five generations; its 80% line is a guide, and Monte Carlo uncertainty remains. The shaded grey region ends at the first estimated crossing, which can vary between estimates.

With “Show gene copies” enabled, two optional dots represent two copies of one neutral marker in a diploid individual. Each colour identifies an allele at that same marker: two matching colours or one of each. The dots are schematic, not physical structures, and do not show all the markers used by the test. Body colour does not encode genetic identity or adaptation.

All calculations run in your browser. The power analysis runs separately so the animals can keep swimming. This is an educational prototype, not validated research software.
